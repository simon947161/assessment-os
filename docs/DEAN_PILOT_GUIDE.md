# Dean Pilot Guide — WHS Living Book v0.1

## Pilot purpose

Mission 01 is a short practice activity for discussing hazard recognition and
safe immediate action in a painting workshop. It is not a formal competency
assessment and does not record learner names.

Expected pilot URL:

<https://simon947161.github.io/assessment-os/>

## Before the session

1. Open the pilot URL on the classroom computer or learner phone.
2. Confirm the landing screen shows **Mission 01 — Spot the Hazards**.
3. If displaying to a group, make the browser large enough for the whole
   workshop scene.
4. Allow approximately 8–10 minutes for one complete mission.

## Suggested introduction

> You are doing the pre-start scan. Find each suspicious location and choose
> the safest immediate action. This is practice, so use the feedback to test
> your thinking.

## Ways to run the pilot

### Individual

Let the learner complete the mission independently. Ask them to explain one
choice they changed after feedback.

### Group screen

Select one location at a time. Ask the group to vote on the safest action
before revealing the feedback.

### Phone

Use the three scene-zone buttons to move between **Access & floor**,
**Active work**, and **Prep & storage**. The accessible location list provides
another way to open every location.

## What to observe

- Can a new learner understand the task within 30 seconds?
- Are any hotspots difficult to find or select?
- Which actions attract the most incorrect first choices?
- Does the feedback explain what remains unsafe?
- Does the language sound natural for painting and decorating learners?
- Are any consequence categories too strong or too weak?
- Can the learner finish without teacher intervention?

## Restart for the next learner

At the debrief, select **Restart for next learner**. This clears the anonymous
mission progress from the current browser session and returns to the landing
screen.

## Feedback to return

Please record:

1. device and group format used;
2. approximate completion time;
3. confusing content or interactions;
4. safety wording that needs correction;
5. the three most valuable learner reactions;
6. whether the pilot should proceed, be revised, or stop.

Do not enter learner names or personal information into the pilot or feedback
record.
